# Layout recipes

Read when composing slides. Coordinates are percentages of the canvas; adapt to content, logo clear space, and the user's platform. These are authored starting recipes inspired by the study, not traced copies.

## A. Numeral + original subject cover

Use for an actual count, not an invented statistic. Put a large numeral in x=6–54%, y=15–65%; a cropped original subject in x=52–100%, y=18–100%; supporting title in x=7–62%, y=64–86%. Keep the top identity and bottom progress quiet. Let the subject overlap the numeral only if the numeral remains recognizable. On content slides, shrink the subject into an edge cameo and use the released area for explanation. For a restrained brand, replace the figure with a relevant product or typographic anchor.

## B. Photographic depth cover

Use one subject near the central axis, with a large headline across the middle/lower-middle and no text over critical facial features. Allocate an intentional readable field; darken that region selectively if necessary. Place two to four blurred, tilted, or receding supporting panels around the subject, leaving the headline undisturbed. Reduce to one evidence panel on content slides. Use perspective consistently rather than tilting every panel differently.

## C. Editorial photo stack

Allocate about 55–65% of width to the central image stack, with a side rail for the index and narrow title. Keep one sharp image dominant. Compress and soften secondary frames toward the top/bottom. Separate the images by scale and perspective, not heavy drop shadows. On a teaching slide, flatten the central frame into a readable evidence image and move explanation into the side region. Do not rotate essential screenshots into illegibility.

## D. Sculptural campaign

Give a single metaphor object roughly the upper 55–70% of the frame, with breathing space around its silhouette. Reserve the remaining area for a headline and brief explanation, either directly on the canvas or in a solid brand-colored panel. Use the same light direction and material family throughout the deck. Change composition and object according to the message. A comparison or process slide can use flat diagrams in the same palette; 3D is not required everywhere.

## E. Comparison that teaches

Put the claim across the upper 20–30%. Place two labeled examples below with comparable scale, clear separation, and an obvious shared axis. Use labels such as “Before / After” or the user's terminology; color is supplementary. Add one short annotation explaining the difference. Keep real evidence distinguishable from illustrative mockups. Shift to one example per slide when side-by-side content becomes unreadable on a phone.

## F. Cinematic poster

Put a close-cropped hero subject in the right 40–55% and tall display type in the left 50–65%, allowing a controlled overlap. One word receives the brand accent. A small stamp can introduce a qualifier; a line or arrow can guide toward the actual promise. Give the final CTA a calmer composition. Use a text-led variation when the brand has no suitable original subject; do not invent a celebrity endorsement.

## Sequence example

For six slides, a possible sequence is: cinematic promise → quiet one-point explanation → labeled comparison → one visual metaphor → concise synthesis → action. For another topic, use a completely different sequence. Repeat identity and visual logic, not a fixed box arrangement. Do not force all recipes into one carousel.
